# Sulu Case Study: Coach Guide

## Key Management Learnings

### 1. Urgency Creates False Dichotomies
**Core Learning:** When under pressure, managers often frame problems as binary choices when multiple creative options exist.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- "Hire quickly" vs. "keep searching" is a false dichotomy
- Urgency narrows thinking—good managers actively resist this
- The best solutions often come from questioning the constraints themselves

**Key Insight:** Sulu is trapped in either/or thinking. Better questions: Can we negotiate the timeline? Break the work into phases? Bring in contractors? Can existing team members take on pieces?

### 2. Hiring Delays Have Compounding Costs
**Core Learning:** Every week without the right person compounds the problem.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Three months passed without a hire—50% of available time lost
- Hiring isn't "in addition to" the work; it IS the work
- Passive recruiting rarely works for specialized roles

**Key Insight:** Sulu should have been proactive from day one: engaging recruiters, leveraging networks, exploring contractors in parallel. The first three months should have included multiple strategies, not just posting and hoping.

### 3. Timeline Commitments Require Risk Assessment
**Core Learning:** When making commitments, managers must explicitly analyze risks and build in buffers.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Sulu's timeline assumes everything goes perfectly
- No buffer for hiring delays, technical complexity, or learning curves
- Commitments should be based on realistic worst-case or expected-case, not best-case

**Key Insight:** A 9-month deadline with 3-month hire search + 3-month execution leaves zero margin. Sulu should have negotiated longer timeline, started hiring immediately, or had contingency plans.

### 4. Communication Failures Amplify Problems
**Core Learning:** Not communicating risks early to stakeholders turns difficult situations into crises.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Leadership likely doesn't know Sulu has been unable to hire for 3 months
- Early escalation creates options; late escalation forces bad choices

**Key Insight:** Sulu should have provided regular updates: "Week 4: No qualified candidates yet. If we don't have someone by Week 8, I recommend X, Y, Z." This gives leadership time to help—potentially opening budget for higher compensation or negotiating timeline.

### 5. Specialization Creates Organizational Vulnerability
**Core Learning:** Teams with unique, non-transferable knowledge create single points of failure.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- The Carboni team's highly specialized nature is both strength and weakness
- Knowledge sharing, documentation, and cross-training reduce this risk

### 6. Perfect Can Be the Enemy of Good (Hiring Edition)
**Core Learning:** Holding out for the "perfect" candidate often means missing great candidates who could succeed with support.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- A motivated learner with strong fundamentals might be better than a domain expert who doesn't fit the team culture

### 7. Scope and Requirements Are Negotiable Until They're Not
**Core Learning:** Technical requirements often have more flexibility than initially assumed.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Scope can often be reduced, phased, or simplified
- MVP thinking applies to government contracts too

### 8. Other Projects and Team Capacity Are Connected
**Core Learning:** Teams don't operate in isolation. Hiring for one project affects all projects.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- The Carboni team has "hard commitments" and revenue targets for IPO
- Adding one new person (who needs training) actually reduces team capacity short-term

## Facilitation Overview

### Session Structure (60-75 minutes)
1. Introduction (5 min)
2. Initial Perspectives (10 min)
3. Timeline Reality Check (15 min)
4. Options Exploration (15-20 min)
5. Root Cause and Prevention (10-15 min)
6. Synthesis and Takeaways (10 min)

---

## Opening the Discussion

**Suggested Opening:**
"Sulu faces a common management challenge: a critical deadline, a specialized skill gap, and a hiring market that isn't cooperating. Let's start by understanding the situation fully before jumping to solutions."

**Initial Poll:** Hire the next strong engineer and train them / Keep searching for a specialist / Something else

---

## Discussion Flow

### Phase 1: Understanding the Timeline (10-15 min)

**Prompt:** "Let's do the math together. Sulu has 6 months. Walk me through what has to happen."

**Build This on Board:**

**Option 1: Hire Generalist**
- Find and close candidate: __ weeks
- Onboarding: __ weeks
- Domain training: 3 months (12 weeks)
- Actual work: __ weeks
- Total: __ weeks. Does this fit in 24 weeks?

**Option 2: Find Specialist**
- Find and close specialist: __ weeks
- Onboarding: __ weeks
- Actual work: 3 months (12 weeks)
- Total: __ weeks. Does this fit in 24 weeks?

**Key Point:** Both options are extremely tight. When both obvious paths seem unlikely to work, we need to either change the paths or change the constraints.

### Phase 2: Questioning Assumptions (15-20 min)

**Prompt:** "Let's identify what Sulu might be assuming that could actually be negotiable."

**Assumptions to Explore:**
- Does ALL the work need to be done for launch?
- Does it have to be one person?
- Is 9 months (now 6) actually fixed?
- Does the person need to be full-time hire?

**Alternative Approaches:**
1. Negotiate Timeline—talk to stakeholders about 6-month reality
2. Reduce Scope—identify true MVP for government initiative
3. Alternative Resourcing—contractors for 6-month engagement
4. Hybrid Approach—hire best available NOW + also bring in contractor
5. Team Reorganization—could existing team members shift priorities?

### Phase 3: The First Three Months (10-15 min)

**Prompt:** "What should Sulu have been doing during those first three months?"

**Week 1-2:** Engage recruiting firms, map candidate market, identify compensation needed
**Week 3-4:** If no strong candidates, escalate to leadership, explore contractor options in parallel
**Week 6-8:** If still no candidates, pivot strategy, communicate risks to leadership
**Week 10-12:** Should have made a decision by now

**Key Learning:** Sulu treated hiring as passive activity. Active hiring includes: proactive sourcing, parallel strategies, regular communication, willingness to pivot quickly.

### Phase 4: Communication to Leadership (10 min)

**Exercise:** "You're Sulu. It's been 3 months. Write the message you should send to your manager."

**What Should Be Included:**
- Current situation (no hire after 3 months)
- Timeline implications (only 6 months left)
- Risks with each option
- What you need from leadership
- Your recommendation

**Key Insight:** Leaders can't help with problems they don't know about. Risk communication isn't admitting failure; it's enabling better decision-making.

### Phase 5: Team Capacity (10 min)

**Prompt:** "The team has 'hard commitments including features needed to hit revenue targets for an upcoming IPO.' How does this affect our thinking?"

**Critical Question:** "What if the right answer is: 'We can't do this project without jeopardizing other commitments'? How would you, as Sulu, make that case?"

---

## Key Teaching Points

✓ Time is a constraint that compounds
✓ Constraints are often more negotiable than they appear
✓ Communication creates options
✓ Perfect is the enemy of good
✓ Portfolio thinking matters
✓ Risk management requires buffers

---

## Handling Difficult Moments

**If Students Fixate on Binary Choice:**
"I notice we keep coming back to 'hire generalist or keep searching.' But we calculated that neither has good odds of success. What if both options are bad?"

**If Students Blame Sulu Harshly:**
"It's easy to see Sulu's mistakes in hindsight. How does a manager avoid falling into this trap?" Redirect to systems and processes, not personal fault.

**If Discussion Stays Too Abstract:**
"You're Sulu. It's Monday morning. What are the first three actions you take?"

---

## Debrief

**Personal Application:** "Where are you making best-case assumptions? Where might you need to communicate risks earlier?"

**Key Takeaways:** "What's the one thing you'll remember from this case when you're facing a deadline crisis?"

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Accepting the binary frame
- Ignoring the timeline math
- Forgetting about other work
- Blaming Sulu without learning
- Assuming hiring is someone else's job
- Treating constraints as fixed

---

*Remember: Your role as facilitator is to guide discovery, not to provide "the answer." The best learning comes from students wrestling with complexity and articulating their own insights.*
