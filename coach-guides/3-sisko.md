# Sisko Case Study: Coach Guide

## Key Management Learnings

This section outlines the critical management concepts and skills that students should develop through discussion of this case study.

### 1. **Manager Roles Must Evolve as Teams Scale**

**Core Learning:** What works for managing 6 people doesn't work for 15. Managers must intentionally evolve their role, not just do more of the same.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Sisko is currently a player-coach (manager + lead engineer)
- This model doesn't scale to 15 people
- Trying to keep the same role while team doubles leads to burnout and bottlenecks
- Role evolution is not failure—it's growth

**Key Insight:** The skills that made Sisko successful at 6 people (deep technical involvement, direct contribution) can become liabilities at 15 people if not adapted. Great managers recognize when to change their approach.

---

### 2. **Span of Control Has Real Limits**

**Core Learning:** Research and experience show managers have optimal spans of control. Exceeding them reduces effectiveness for everyone.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Typical effective span: 5-8 direct reports for hands-on management
- Can stretch to 10-12 for very senior, independent teams
- Beyond that, something must change (structure, role, support)
- Quality of management decreases as span increases beyond limits

**Key Insight:** Sisko managing 15 people directly means shallow relationships, reactive management, and limited development focus. Either the team needs substructure (tech leads, team leads) or Sisko needs to manage managers, not individual contributors.

---

### 3. **Team Structure Should Follow the Work**

**Core Learning:** Organizational structure should be designed around the actual work patterns, not generic org-chart templates.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Banks team does bespoke integrations, each requiring ongoing maintenance
- Work could organize by: customer/bank, technology layer, new vs. maintenance, geographic region
- Different structures enable different strengths (specialization vs. flexibility)
- Structure is a tool to make work easier, not an end in itself

**Key Insight:** Sisko should ask: "How does work actually flow?" "Where are handoffs?" "What requires coordination?" The answers reveal natural team boundaries. For example, if each bank integration is independent, customer-aligned pods make sense. If there's shared infrastructure, a platform team might help.

---

### 4. **Delegation Is a Skill, Not Just Dumping Work**

**Core Learning:** Effective delegation means developing others to take ownership, not just assigning tasks.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Sisko can't remain lead engineer while managing 15 people
- Delegation requires: identifying what to delegate, who to delegate to, how to support them
- Good delegation develops people and creates organizational capacity
- Poor delegation creates stress and resentment

**Key Insight:** Sisko needs to develop other technical leaders. This means intentional investment in others' growth, gradual handoff with support, and being comfortable with different approaches. The goal is "they own it" not "they do what I would do."

---

### 5. **Growth Phases Have Predictable Inflection Points**

**Core Learning:** Team scaling isn't linear. Certain sizes trigger qualitative changes in how teams must operate.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- 1-6: Manager can be hands-on, know everything, direct communication works
- 7-12: Need more structure; manager becomes coordinator; delegation essential
- 12+: Often requires management layers; manager focuses on managers/leads
- Each inflection requires conscious role adjustment

**Key Insight:** Sisko is about to hit a major inflection (6→15). This isn't just "more of the same." The team will need different structure, different communication patterns, different decision-making. Sisko should plan for this proactively, not react when things break.

---

### 6. **Player-Coach Model Has an Expiration Date**

**Core Learning:** The player-coach model (manager who is also a significant IC) works in specific contexts but doesn't scale indefinitely.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Works well: small teams (3-6), highly experienced teams, stable environments
- Breaks down: larger teams, high-growth phases, teams needing development
- Manager's IC work can become a bottleneck or distraction
- Transition from player-coach to full-time manager is common and healthy

**Key Insight:** Sisko must decide: Do I transition to full-time management? Do I develop a lead engineer to take my IC role? Do I stay player-coach with strong team leads handling management? Each choice has implications. The key is making the choice consciously, not drifting.

---

### 7. **Asking for Support Is Strategic Leadership**

**Core Learning:** Knowing what support you need and asking for it is a strength, not a weakness.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Sisko has "wide latitude" to shape growth
- This includes asking for resources, training, structural support
- Leadership often can't help if they don't know what's needed
- Specific requests are more effective than vague concerns

**Key Insight:** Sisko should think: "What do I need to be successful at 15 people?" Possibilities: management training, coach/mentor, recruiting support, budget for team leads, tools/systems for coordination. Good leaders ask for what they need before they're drowning.

---

### 8. **Structure Enables or Constrains Performance**

**Core Learning:** How you organize the team affects what the team can accomplish. Structure is a strategic choice.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Flat structure: fast decision-making, but high coordination overhead
- Hierarchical structure: clearer accountability, but slower and more rigid
- Pod/squad structure: autonomy and ownership, but potential duplication
- Each structure optimizes for different things

**Key Insight:** There's no universally "best" structure. Sisko should choose based on: the work (bespoke integrations suggest customer-aligned pods might work), the people (seniority and experience levels), and the goals (more parallel integrations vs. better maintenance). Structure should be revisited as these factors change.

---

## Facilitation Overview

### Session Structure (Recommended: 60-75 minutes)

1. **Introduction** (5 min)
   - Frame the case and learning objectives
   - Set discussion norms

2. **Current State Analysis** (10 min)
   - What is Sisko doing now?
   - What works at 6 people?

3. **Growth Patterns Exploration** (20 min)
   - Different structural options
   - Tradeoffs and fit with work

4. **Role Evolution** (15 min)
   - How Sisko's role must change
   - Skills to develop or delegate

5. **Inflection Points and Support** (10 min)
   - When does role shift?
   - What support is needed?

6. **Synthesis and Takeaways** (10 min)
   - Key learnings
   - Application to students' contexts

---

## Facilitation Guide

### Opening the Discussion

**Goal:** Establish that growth requires intentional design, not just "more people"

**Suggested Opening:**
> "Sisko has what sounds like a great opportunity: budget to grow from 6 to 15 people! But growth isn't just addition—it often requires transformation. What changes when a team more than doubles?"

**Initial Question:**
- "By show of hands: Have you been part of a team that grew significantly?"
- "What changed as it grew? What got harder? What got better?"

*Capture a few experiences to ground the discussion in reality.*

---

### Discussion Flow

#### Phase 1: Understanding Current State (10 min)

**Prompt:** "Let's start by understanding where Sisko is now. The case says he's 'doubling as the lead engineer.' What does that tell us?"

**Guided Questions:**
- "What does Sisko probably spend his time on?"
- "What are the advantages of this player-coach model?"
- "Why does this work at 6 people?"

**Create a "Day in the Life" Snapshot:**

**Sisko's Current Role (at 6 people):**
- Technical work: Writing code, architecture decisions, code review
- People management: 1:1s, hiring, performance management
- Project management: Planning, coordination with customers
- Lead engineer: Deep technical decision-making

**What Makes This Viable at 6 People:**
- Can have meaningful 1:1s with everyone
- Can stay close to all technical work
- Team is small enough to communicate directly
- Sisko's technical expertise is differentiator

**Key Question:**
> "Now imagine this at 15 people. What breaks?"

**Answers to Surface:**
- 15 x 1 hour biweekly 1:1s = 7.5 hours every two weeks (almost 1 full day)
- Can't review all code or be in all technical decisions
- Too much coordination overhead
- Sisko becomes bottleneck
- No time for strategic thinking

---

#### Phase 2: Growth Patterns Exploration (20 min)

**Prompt:** "Let's explore different ways to structure a 15-person team. There's no single right answer, but different patterns have different tradeoffs."

**Facilitate Exploration of Patterns:**

**Option 1: Flat Team (Sisko manages all 15)**

| **Pros** | **Cons** |
|----------|----------|
| • No additional structure/overhead | • Sisko spread extremely thin |
| • Everyone has direct access to manager | • Shallow relationships with each person |
| • Clear reporting structure | • Sisko is bottleneck for decisions |
| • Simple to understand | • No technical depth from Sisko |

**When this works:** Very senior, independent team needing minimal management

**Option 2: Tech Leads Model (Sisko manages 15, with 2-3 tech leads)**

| **Pros** | **Cons** |
|----------|----------|
| • Technical leadership distributed | • Tech leads have unclear authority |
| • Sisko retains direct relationships | • Can create confusion about decision rights |
| • Develops future leaders | • Tech leads do extra work without formal role |
| • Technical decisions don't bottleneck on Sisko | • May not reduce Sisko's management load much |

**When this works:** Team needs strong technical leadership; not ready for formal sub-teams

**Option 3: Team Leads Model (Sisko manages 3 team leads, they manage ~4-5 each)**

| **Pros** | **Cons** |
|----------|----------|
| • Sisko has manageable span (3 people) | • Adds management layer |
| • Team leads own people development | • Sisko loses direct relationship with ICs |
| • Scales beyond 15 easily | • Requires people who want to manage |
| • Clear accountability | • Communication across teams needs attention |

**When this works:** Team will keep growing; people ready for management; work can subdivide

**Option 4: Pod/Squad Model (3-4 small teams, each with a lead)**

| **Pros** | **Cons** |
|----------|----------|
| • Organized around customers/domains | • Potential duplication of effort |
| • Ownership and autonomy | • Coordination across pods challenging |
| • Can move fast | • May need shared platform/infrastructure team |
| • Natural accountability | • Requires careful work division |

**When this works:** Work naturally divides (e.g., by bank/customer); value autonomy over efficiency

**Guided Questions for Each:**
- "How would this align with the Banks team's work?"
- "What does this require from Sisko? From team members?"
- "What problems does this solve? What problems does it create?"

**Critical Discussion:**
> "Notice that each pattern is a tool. The question isn't 'which is best' but 'which best fits the work, the people, and the goals?' What would help us decide?"

**Factors to Consider:**
- Nature of work (bespoke integrations, ongoing maintenance)
- Could organize by customer/bank, by technical layer, by new vs. maintenance
- Team member seniority and desire for leadership
- Future growth plans (will it keep growing beyond 15?)
- Sisko's strengths and preferences

---

#### Phase 3: Role Evolution Deep Dive (15 min)

**Prompt:** "However Sisko structures the team, his role must change. Let's map that evolution."

**Build This Together:**

**From → To Mapping:**

| **What Sisko Does Now (6 people)** | **What Sisko Should Do at 15** | **Who Handles Old Responsibilities** |
|-----------------------------------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Lead engineer, deep technical work | Technical strategy and architecture | Tech leads or senior engineers |
| Code review for most code | Review critical pieces, develop reviewers | Distributed to team |
| All customer relationships | Strategic relationships, delegates tactical | Team leads or account owners |
| All hiring decisions | Hiring strategy, delegate interviews | Team leads participate heavily |
| All 1:1s personally | 1:1s with leads, skip-levels with ICs | Team leads have regular 1:1s |

**Guided Questions:**
- "What's hard about this transition for Sisko?"
- "What might Sisko need to learn or develop?"
- "How does Sisko ensure quality when he's not reviewing every PR?"

**Common Challenges to Surface:**
- **Letting go**: Hard to stop doing things you're good at and enjoy
- **Trust**: Concern that others won't do it "right"
- **Identity**: "I'm a great engineer" → "I enable great engineering"
- **Control**: Delegation means accepting different approaches
- **Pace**: Developing others takes time upfront

**Key Teaching Point:**
> "This transition—from doing to enabling—is one of the hardest parts of becoming a senior manager. How do you know you're doing it well?"

**Answers to Draw Out:**
- Team delivers without you being in the details
- Others are growing and taking ownership
- You have time for strategic thinking
- Team can function well when you're away (like if you took a sabbatical)

---

#### Phase 4: Inflection Points and Timing (10 min)

**Prompt:** "Growth doesn't happen all at once. Sisko will go from 6 to 7 to 8... to 15. When does the role need to shift?"

**Discussion Questions:**
- "At what team size does Sisko need to stop being lead engineer?"
- "At what size does Sisko need team leads or managers?"
- "Should Sisko change his role before it breaks, or wait and respond?"

**Build Inflection Timeline:**

**6-8 people:**
- Sisko can still be player-coach
- Start identifying future tech leads
- Begin delegating some technical decisions

**9-12 people:**
- Player-coach model getting strained
- Need clear tech leads or transition to full-time management
- Communication patterns need more structure

**13-15 people:**
- Must have team leads or sub-teams
- Sisko focused on management, strategy, coordination
- Can't be in all the details

**Key Insight:**
"Notice these changes should be proactive, not reactive. What happens if Sisko waits until he's drowning to make changes?"

**Answer:** Crisis management, poor decisions, burnout, team suffers. Better to plan transitions before they're forced.

---

#### Phase 5: Support Needs and Asks (10 min)

**Prompt:** "The case says Sisko has 'wide latitude' in shaping growth. What should he ask for from Acme leadership?"

**Brainstorm Support Needs:**

**Training and Development:**
- Management training for scaling teams
- Coach or mentor who has managed large teams
- Leadership development for potential tech leads

**Resources:**
- Recruiting support for rapid hiring
- Onboarding program for new team members
- Tools for team communication and coordination

**Structural:**
- Budget for team lead roles (if compensation differs)
- Approval for subteam structure
- Clarity on authority and decision rights

**Strategic:**
- Alignment on priorities (new integrations vs. maintenance)
- Roadmap clarity for annual planning
- Connection to peer managers for learning

**Discussion:**
- "Which of these are most critical for Sisko?"
- "How should Sisko frame these requests?"
- "What's the difference between asking for support and complaining?"

**Key Teaching Point:**
Effective leaders are specific about what they need: "To successfully grow to 15 people while maintaining quality and team culture, I need: (1) budget for 2-3 team lead roles, (2) a management coach to help me navigate the transition, and (3) dedicated recruiting support for 6 months."

---

### Handling Difficult Moments

#### If Students Suggest Sisko Can Stay Player-Coach

**Intervention:**
> "Let's do the math. Fifteen 1-hour 1:1s every two weeks is how many hours? Plus hiring, performance reviews, planning, customer escalations... Can Sisko also be lead engineer producing significant code? What gets sacrificed?"

#### If Discussion Stays Too Abstract

**Intervention:**
> "Let's get concrete. Imagine you're Sisko. Tomorrow you have 10 people, next month 13, in 3 months 15. What specifically changes in your day-to-day work? What do you stop doing?"

#### If Students Assume One Structure Is Always Right

**Intervention:**
> "I'm hearing strong advocacy for [structure X]. That might be right for Banks team. But could you imagine situations where a different structure would be better? What factors make the difference?"

*Guide toward contingent thinking: the right answer depends on context.*

#### If Students Don't Address the People Side

**Intervention:**
> "We've talked a lot about org charts. But what about the humans involved? How does Sisko help his current team through this change? How does he set up new team members for success?"

## Key Teaching Points to Ensure Coverage

Make sure these concepts emerge during discussion:

### ✓ **Scaling requires role evolution**
- What works at 6 doesn't work at 15
- Manager role must intentionally change
- From doing to enabling

### ✓ **Span of control matters**
- Research-backed limits exist
- Quality degrades beyond optimal span
- Structure helps manage span

### ✓ **Structure follows work**
- Organize around how work actually flows
- Different structures optimize for different things
- Should revisit as work changes

### ✓ **Delegation is skill**
- Not just assigning tasks
- Developing others to own areas
- Requires trust and letting go

### ✓ **Inflection points are predictable**
- Team sizes trigger qualitative changes
- Better to plan ahead than react
- Proactive transitions smoother than reactive

### ✓ **Asking for support is leadership**
- Know what you need
- Be specific in requests
- Leaders can't help if they don't know

## Debrief and Synthesis (10 min)

### Closing Questions

**Personal Application:**
> "Think about your own role. As your team or scope grows, how does your role need to evolve? What might you need to let go of?"

**Key Takeaways:**
> "What's the most important thing you learned about scaling teams from this discussion?"

**Commitment to Action:**
> "If you're managing a growing team, what's one thing you'll do differently based on this conversation?"

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### ❌ **Assuming Growth Is Just Addition**
Growth changes the system, not just the size.

### ❌ **Treating All Structures as Equally Valid**
Push students to match structure to actual work.

### ❌ **Ignoring the Emotional/Identity Challenge**
Sisko's transition involves letting go of technical identity.

### ❌ **Forgetting About Existing Team Members**
They're experiencing change too; how are they supported?

### ❌ **Not Considering the Work Itself**
Bespoke integrations suggest certain structures make more sense.

### ❌ **Skipping the Details of Transition**
How exactly does Sisko move from current to future state?

## Assessment: What Good Discussion Looks Like

Students are learning well if they:

- ✅ Recognize that Sisko's role must fundamentally change
- ✅ Explore multiple structural options with tradeoffs
- ✅ Consider how work type influences structure
- ✅ Discuss delegation and development of others
- ✅ Identify inflection points at specific team sizes
- ✅ Think about proactive vs. reactive transitions
- ✅ Consider what support Sisko needs
- ✅ Connect to their own experiences with growing teams

## Follow-Up Activities (Optional)

### Writing Exercise
Ask students to write:
1. Sisko's annual plan for growth (one-page summary)
2. Job descriptions for team lead roles Sisko might create
3. A transition plan: "What I'll stop doing and who will do it"

### Small Group Work
Divide into groups:
- Group A: Design a pod structure organized by customer
- Group B: Design a hierarchical structure with team leads
- Group C: Design a flat structure with strong tech leads
- Each presents their design and explains the tradeoffs

### Role-Play Exercise
Set up conversations:
- Person 1: Sisko, Person 2: Leadership (presenting the growth plan)
- Person 1: Sisko, Person 2: Current team member (explaining changes)
