# Kira Case Study: Coach Guide

## Key Management Learnings

### 1. Trust Is Earned Through Transparency and Delivered Commitments
**Core Learning:** Trust breaks when commitments aren't met. It's rebuilt through honest communication, realistic expectations, and delivering on promises—not through explanations or justifications.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Kira (and team) missed "several key deadlines"
- Each miss erodes trust with Dax and executives
- Explanations don't rebuild trust; delivery does
- Trust is rebuilt incrementally through small, kept promises

**Key Insight:** Dax doesn't trust Kira's explanations because past explanations preceded more delays. "We're working on it" has lost credibility. Kira needs to shift from explaining why things are late to delivering something—anything—that demonstrates progress.

### 2. Escalation Indicates Communication Failure
**Core Learning:** When stakeholders escalate, it's rarely because they're unreasonable. It's usually because they don't trust the information they're getting or the path forward.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Dax escalates because he doesn't trust Kira's updates
- "Relaxing pressure could lead to further delays" suggests Dax believes pressure is the only thing driving progress
- Escalation is a symptom of broken communication and trust
- Responding to escalation with more explanations doesn't work

**Key Insight:** Dax's escalation says "I don't believe this will get fixed without executive intervention." Kira needs to understand why Dax feels this way and what he actually needs.

### 3. Burnout and Missed Deadlines Signal Systemic Problems
**Core Learning:** When highly motivated teams work hard but miss deadlines and approach burnout, the problem isn't effort—it's systemic issues like poor planning, unclear requirements, or misaligned expectations.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Helios team is "highly motivated" and "deeply committed"
- They're at "brink of burnout"
- Still missing deadlines despite intense effort
- This pattern indicates: unrealistic commitments, unclear requirements, poor planning, or missing capabilities

**Key Insight:** You can't motivate your way out of systemic problems. Working harder won't fix bad estimates, unclear requirements, or architectural issues. Kira and Riker need to diagnose the systemic issues.

### 4. Adding Resources to Late Projects Often Makes Them Later
**Core Learning:** Brooks's Law: Adding people to a late software project makes it later. Onboarding overhead and communication complexity slow productive team members.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Kira tried "adding engineers to the team"
- "Steep ramp-up time has delayed any positive impact"
- New engineers need training, context, code review from existing team
- This takes time away from delivery

**Key Insight:** When a project is late, adding people is tempting but often wrong. For Helios, with "deep technical expertise" and "long ramp-up," this was predictably ineffective. Better: reduce scope, extend timeline, remove blockers.

### 5. Commitments Without Understanding Create Inevitable Failure
**Core Learning:** Making commitments before understanding dependencies and complexity creates a no-win situation. Either you fail to deliver or you burn out the team trying.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Work involves "managing dependencies that were not fully understood when commitments were made"
- This is root cause of the crisis
- Someone committed to regulatory agencies without full technical understanding
- Now team pays the price

**Key Insight:** The case notes "dependencies not fully understood when commitments were made." This is the original sin. Commitments should be made WITH engineering input, not imposed ON engineering.

### 6. Pass/Fail Framing Prevents Productive Problem-Solving
**Core Learning:** When projects are framed as binary (success or failure), stakeholders dig in rather than collaborate. Spectrum thinking enables better outcomes.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Case asks: "How might Kira engage Dax without framing as pass/fail?"
- Pass/fail creates: defensive behavior, blame, escalation, all-or-nothing thinking
- Spectrum thinking creates: collaboration, creativity, phased approaches, realistic planning

**Key Insight:** Current framing is likely: "Will Aurora be ready: yes or no?" Better: "What compliance capabilities can we demonstrate by [date]? What would come in Phase 2? What would make regulators most confident?"

### 7. Leaders Must Balance Stakeholder Pressure and Team Protection
**Core Learning:** Directors like Kira must manage up (to executives and stakeholders) while protecting and supporting teams. This requires absorbing pressure, not transmitting it.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Kira is caught between burned-out team and escalating stakeholder
- Simply passing pressure down doesn't help (team already motivated and overworked)
- Simply explaining up doesn't help (Dax doesn't trust it)
- Kira must actively manage both directions

**Key Insight:** Kira's job is not just to report status. It's to: (1) protect team from unrealistic pressure while holding them accountable, (2) educate stakeholders about reality, (3) negotiate realistic paths forward, (4) make trade-offs explicit.

### 8. Long-term Sustainability Requires Short-term Relief
**Core Learning:** You can't build sustainable practices while in crisis mode. Teams need breathing room to establish healthy patterns.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Case asks about "long-term sustainability while addressing immediate crisis"
- Can't do both if both demand 100% effort
- Must create space for sustainable practices by addressing crisis through scope, timeline, or resource negotiation
- Burnout prevention requires actual workload reduction

**Key Insight:** If team continues at current pace, they'll burn out or quit. Kira must create relief: negotiate timeline extension, reduce scope, defer other work. Only with breathing room can team establish sustainable practices.

## Facilitation Overview

### Session Structure (60-75 minutes)
1. Introduction (5 min)
2. Understanding the Situation (10 min)
3. Trust Breakdown Analysis (15 min)
4. Root Cause Exploration (10-15 min)
5. Rebuilding Trust and Supporting Team (15 min)
6. Synthesis and Takeaways (10 min)

---

## Opening the Discussion

**Suggested Opening:**
"Kira faces one of the hardest challenges in engineering leadership: a burned-out team missing deadlines, a stakeholder who doesn't trust her and keeps escalating, and executives who just want it fixed. She's tried adding engineers and explaining details. Neither worked. What should she do?"

**Initial Poll:** "Who do you empathize with most: Kira, Riker, the team, or Dax? Why?"

---

## Discussion Flow

### Phase 1: Mapping the Situation (10 min)

**Build Timeline:**
- Commitments made to regulatory agencies (by Dax)
- Aurora development begins
- Deadlines missed (multiple times)
- Dax escalates to executives
- Kira adds engineers → doesn't help
- Kira explains to Dax → he keeps escalating
- Executives tell Kira to "just fix it"
- Team approaching burnout
- Present: Kira must rebuild trust and support team

**Key Players and Their Pressures:**
- Helios team: Working hard, burning out, missing deadlines
- Riker: Managing burned-out team under pressure
- Kira: Director caught between team and stakeholders
- Dax: Made commitments to regulators, can't deliver, keeps escalating
- Executives: Want it fixed
- Regulatory agencies: Expecting compliance demonstration

**Discussion:** "Everyone has legitimate concerns and pressures. This isn't heroes and villains. What makes this so hard?"

**Answer:** Multiple valid but competing pressures. Team needs relief. Dax needs delivery. Executives need confidence. Regulators need compliance.

### Phase 2: Trust Breakdown Analysis (15 min)

**Prompt:** "The case centers on 'trust breakdown' between Kira and Dax. Let's understand how trust broke down."

**How Trust Broke:**
1. Commitments made (possibly without full engineering input)
2. First deadline missed
3. Explanation given, new commitment made
4. Second deadline missed
5. More explanations, less credibility
6. Pattern: miss → explain → miss → explain
7. Dax stops trusting explanations, starts escalating

**Why "Explaining Details" Doesn't Work:**
- Trust isn't rebuilt through words, but through actions
- Dax has heard explanations before, then seen more delays
- Explanations feel like excuses
- Dax needs confidence in path forward, not understanding of past

**Why Dax Keeps Escalating:**
- Believes "relaxing pressure could lead to further delays"
- May think pressure is only thing driving progress
- Doesn't trust the current path
- Faces own pressures from regulatory agencies

**Critical Question:** "What would rebuild trust with Dax? Not explanations—what would actually rebuild trust?"

**Answers:**
- Delivering something (anything) on time
- Being transparently realistic about what's possible
- Involving Dax in trade-off discussions
- Showing Dax the work and progress directly
- Making smaller commitments and keeping them
- Acknowledging past failures honestly

### Phase 3: Root Cause Exploration (10-15 min)

**Prompt:** "The team is highly motivated but missing deadlines and approaching burnout. What's really causing this?"

**Build Root Cause Analysis:**

**Surface Symptom:** Missing deadlines

**Contributing Factors:**
- Work more complex than estimated?
- Dependencies not understood?
- Requirements changing?
- Technical blockers?
- Unrealistic commitments?

**Deeper Root Causes:**
- "Dependencies not fully understood when commitments were made"
- Who made commitments? With what input?
- Was there estimation process?

**The Brooks's Law Problem:**
- Kira added engineers
- "Steep ramp-up time" means they slow team down
- What does it tell us that it didn't work?

**The Burnout Signal:**
- Team "highly motivated" and "deeply committed"
- "Brink of burnout"
- This says: problem isn't motivation, it's systemic

**Key Insight:** This isn't a motivation or effort problem. It's systemic: commitments were made without full understanding, creating an impossible situation. Adding more effort or people won't fix it.

### Phase 4: Failed Solutions Analysis (10 min)

**Prompt:** "Kira tried two things that didn't work. Let's understand why they failed."

**Solution 1: Adding Engineers**
- Why reasonable: More people = more capacity
- Why it failed: Steep ramp-up, Brooks's Law, doesn't address systemic issues
- What this teaches: Can't just throw bodies at the problem

**Solution 2: Explaining Details to Dax**
- Why reasonable: If Dax understood complexity, he'd be more patient
- Why it failed: Trust already broken, explanations feel like excuses
- What this teaches: Can't explain your way out of broken trust

**Critical Question:** "If both approaches failed, what should Kira try instead?"

### Phase 5: Rebuilding Trust and Supporting Team (15 min)

**Prompt:** "What should Kira and Riker actually do?"

**Immediate Actions (This Week):**

**1. Create Honest Conversation with Dax**
- Acknowledge: "Trust is broken. We've missed deadlines."
- Be transparent: "Here's what we understand now."
- Involve Dax in trade-offs: "We can deliver X by [date], Y by [later date], or partial Z. What's most important to regulatory agencies?"
- Shift from defending to problem-solving together

**2. Reset Expectations with Executives**
- "Just fix it" isn't actionable
- Present: "Here's the situation. Here are options with trade-offs. Here's my recommendation. I need your support."
- Make trade-offs explicit: scope, quality, timeline—pick two

**3. Give Team Breathing Room**
- Communicate: "We're resetting expectations. You don't need to work nights and weekends."
- Reduce scope or defer other work
- Show team their leadership is managing stakeholders

**Medium-term Actions:**

**4. Deliver Something Small**
- Identify quick win that demonstrates progress
- Make specific, small commitment and deliver on time
- Build credibility incrementally

**5. Increase Transparency**
- Show Dax actual progress regularly (demos, sprint reviews)
- Make work visible, not just reported
- Builds empathy and understanding

**6. Address Root Causes**
- How were initial commitments made?
- Fix this pattern for future projects

**The Pass/Fail Reframing:**

**Current Framing (Doesn't Work):**
- "Will Aurora be ready for regulatory review: yes or no?"
- Creates defensive, blame-oriented dynamic

**Better Framing:**
- "What audit capabilities can we demonstrate by [date]?"
- "What would make regulators most confident?"
- "If we phase this, what's critical first phase?"
- Creates collaborative, problem-solving dynamic

## Key Teaching Points

✓ Trust rebuilt through delivery, not explanation
✓ Escalation indicates communication failure
✓ Burnout + missed deadlines = systemic problem
✓ Adding people to late projects backfires
✓ Commitments without understanding create failure
✓ Pass/fail framing prevents problem-solving
✓ Leaders must manage in multiple directions
✓ Sustainability requires breathing room

---

## Handling Difficult Moments

**If Students Blame Dax for Unreasonable Escalation:**
"Dax made commitments to regulatory agencies. His credibility is on the line. He's tried trusting Kira's explanations. From his perspective, what choice does he have?"

**If Students Think More Pressure Will Help:**
"The team is already 'highly motivated' and at 'brink of burnout.' What happens if we add more pressure? Can you motivate people into working harder than they already are?"

**If Students Suggest Just Meeting the Deadline:**
"If it were possible to just meet the deadline, wouldn't they have done it? What constraints exist? What would need to change?"

---

## Debrief

**Personal Application:** "Have you been in a situation where trust broke down with a stakeholder? What worked or didn't work to rebuild it?"

**Key Takeaways:** "What's most important about managing stakeholder relationships and team health from this case?"

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Blaming Dax for unreasonable escalation
- Thinking more pressure will help
- Accepting "just fix it" as reasonable direction
- Missing the Brooks's Law lesson
- Not addressing root cause
- Ignoring burnout implications

---

*Remember: Your role as facilitator is to guide discovery, not to provide "the answer." The best learning comes from students wrestling with complexity and articulating their own insights.*
