# Kira Case Study: Coach Guide

## Key Management Learnings

This section outlines the critical management concepts and skills that students should develop through discussion of this case study.

### 1. **Trust Is Earned Through Transparency and Delivered Commitments**

**Core Learning:** Trust breaks when commitments aren't met. It's rebuilt through honest communication, realistic expectations, and delivering on promises—not through explanations or justifications.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Kira (and team) missed "several key deadlines"
- Each miss erodes trust with Dax and executives
- Explanations don't rebuild trust; delivery does
- Trust is rebuilt incrementally through small, kept promises

**Key Insight:** Dax doesn't trust Kira's explanations because past explanations preceded more delays. "We're working on it" has lost credibility. Kira needs to shift from explaining why things are late to delivering something—anything—that demonstrates progress. Small wins rebuild trust better than perfect explanations.

---

### 2. **Escalation Indicates Communication Failure**

**Core Learning:** When stakeholders escalate, it's rarely because they're unreasonable. It's usually because they don't trust the information they're getting or the path forward.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Dax escalates because he doesn't trust Kira's updates
- "Relaxing pressure could lead to further delays" suggests Dax believes pressure is the only thing driving progress
- Escalation is a symptom of broken communication and trust
- Responding to escalation with more explanations doesn't work

**Key Insight:** Dax's escalation says "I don't believe this will get fixed without executive intervention." Kira needs to understand why Dax feels this way. Is the information unclear? Does Dax not understand technical complexity? Does Dax have his own pressures (regulatory agencies) that he's not sharing? The escalation is information about the relationship, not just about the project.

---

### 3. **Burnout and Missed Deadlines Signal Systemic Problems**

**Core Learning:** When highly motivated teams work hard but miss deadlines and approach burnout, the problem isn't effort—it's systemic issues like poor planning, unclear requirements, or misaligned expectations.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Helios team is "highly motivated" and "deeply committed"
- They're at "brink of burnout"
- Still missing deadlines despite intense effort
- This pattern indicates: unrealistic commitments, unclear requirements, poor planning, or missing capabilities

**Key Insight:** You can't motivate your way out of systemic problems. The team is already motivated. Working harder won't fix bad estimates, unclear requirements, or architectural issues. Kira and Riker need to diagnose the systemic issues: Were commitments realistic? Are requirements clear? Do we have the right approach? Are there technical blockers not being addressed?

---

### 4. **Adding Resources to Late Projects Often Makes Them Later**

**Core Learning:** Brooks's Law: Adding people to a late software project makes it later. Onboarding overhead and communication complexity slow productive team members.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Kira tried "adding engineers to the team"
- "Steep ramp-up time for new members has delayed any positive impact"
- New engineers need training, context, code review from existing team
- This takes time away from delivery, making things slower

**Key Insight:** When a project is late, adding people is tempting but often wrong. The team must absorb new members, slowing current work. For Helios, with "deep technical expertise" and "long ramp-up period," this was predictably ineffective. Better options: reduce scope, extend timeline, remove blockers, improve tooling, or bring in specialized help for specific bottlenecks.

---

### 5. **Commitments Without Understanding Create Inevitable Failure**

**Core Learning:** Making commitments before understanding dependencies and complexity creates a no-win situation. Either you fail to deliver or you burn out the team trying.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Work involves "managing dependencies that were not fully understood when commitments were made"
- This is root cause of the crisis
- Someone (Dax? Kira? Riker?) committed to regulatory agencies without full technical understanding
- Now team pays the price trying to meet unrealistic commitments

**Key Insight:** The case notes "dependencies that were not fully understood when commitments were made." This is the original sin. Who made these commitments? Were engineers consulted? Was there estimation process? This pattern must be fixed to prevent future crises: commitments should be made WITH engineering input, not imposed ON engineering.

---

### 6. **Pass/Fail Framing Prevents Productive Problem-Solving**

**Core Learning:** When projects are framed as binary (success or failure), stakeholders dig in rather than collaborate. Spectrum thinking ("what can we deliver by when?") enables better outcomes.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Case asks: "How might Kira and Riker engage Dax and other stakeholders to manage expectations without framing the project as a pass/fail scenario?"
- Pass/fail framing creates: defensive behavior, blame, escalation, all-or-nothing thinking
- Spectrum framing creates: collaboration, creativity, phased approaches, realistic planning

**Key Insight:** Current framing is likely: "Will Aurora be ready for regulatory demonstration: yes or no?" This creates pressure but not progress. Better framing: "What compliance capabilities can we demonstrate by [date]? What would come in Phase 2? What would make regulators most confident?" This invites problem-solving rather than blame.

---

### 7. **Leaders Must Balance Stakeholder Pressure and Team Protection**

**Core Learning:** Directors like Kira must manage up (to executives and stakeholders) while protecting and supporting teams. This requires absorbing pressure, not transmitting it.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Kira is caught between burned-out team and escalating stakeholder
- Simply passing pressure down to team doesn't help (already motivated and overworked)
- Simply explaining up to Dax doesn't help (he doesn't trust it)
- Kira must actively manage both directions

**Key Insight:** Kira's job is not just to report status. It's to: (1) protect team from unrealistic pressure while holding them accountable, (2) educate stakeholders about reality while maintaining credibility, (3) negotiate realistic paths forward, (4) make trade-offs explicit, and (5) take responsibility for outcomes. "Just fix it" from executives isn't helpful, but Kira shouldn't just pass that pressure to Riker and team.

---

### 8. **Long-term Sustainability Requires Short-term Relief**

**Core Learning:** You can't build sustainable practices while in crisis mode. Teams need breathing room to establish healthy patterns.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Case asks about "long-term sustainability for Helios while addressing the immediate crisis"
- Can't do both simultaneously if both demand 100% effort
- Must create space for sustainable practices by addressing crisis through scope, timeline, or resource negotiation
- Burnout prevention requires actual workload reduction, not just wellness programs

**Key Insight:** If team continues at current pace, they'll burn out or quit. Kira must create relief: negotiate timeline extension, reduce scope, defer other work, bring in targeted help, or acknowledge some commitments can't be met. Only with breathing room can team establish sustainable practices, improve tooling, pay down technical debt, or improve processes.

---

## Facilitation Overview

### Session Structure (Recommended: 60-75 minutes)

1. **Introduction** (5 min)
   - Frame the case and learning objectives
   - Set discussion norms

2. **Understanding the Situation** (10 min)
   - What's happened?
   - Who are the players?
   - What's the core problem?

3. **Trust Breakdown Analysis** (15 min)
   - How did trust break down?
   - Why does Dax keep escalating?
   - What doesn't work?

4. **Root Cause Exploration** (10-15 min)
   - Why is team missing deadlines?
   - What systemic issues exist?
   - What created this situation?

5. **Rebuilding Trust and Supporting Team** (15 min)
   - How to engage Dax differently?
   - How to support burned-out team?
   - Short-term vs. long-term actions

6. **Synthesis and Takeaways** (10 min)
   - Key learnings
   - Application to students' contexts

## Facilitation Guide

### Opening the Discussion

**Goal:** Surface the complexity of managing multiple stakeholders under pressure

**Suggested Opening:**
> "Kira faces one of the hardest challenges in engineering leadership: a burned-out team missing deadlines, a stakeholder who doesn't trust her and keeps escalating, and executives who just want it fixed. She's tried adding engineers and explaining details. Neither worked. What should she do?"

**Initial Poll:**
- "Who do you empathize with most in this case: Kira, Riker, the team, or Dax?"
- "Why?"

*This reveals different perspectives students are bringing and helps establish that everyone has legitimate concerns.*

---

### Discussion Flow

#### Phase 1: Mapping the Situation (10 min)

**Prompt:** "Let's make sure we understand what's happened and who's involved."

**Build Timeline:**
- Commitments made to regulatory agencies (by Dax)
- Aurora development begins
- Deadlines missed (multiple times)
- Dax escalates to executives
- Kira adds engineers → doesn't help
- Kira explains to Dax → he keeps escalating
- Executives tell Kira to "just fix it"
- Team approaching burnout
- Present: Kira must rebuild trust and support team

**Key Players and Their Pressures:**
- **Helios team**: Working hard, burning out, missing deadlines despite effort
- **Riker**: Managing burned-out team under pressure
- **Kira**: Director caught between team and stakeholders
- **Dax**: Made commitments to regulators, can't deliver, keeps escalating
- **Executives**: Want it fixed, told Kira to "just fix it"
- **Regulatory agencies**: Expecting compliance demonstration

**Discussion:**
> "Everyone here has legitimate concerns and pressures. This isn't a story of heroes and villains. What makes this situation so hard?"

**Answer to Surface:** Multiple valid but competing pressures. Team needs relief. Dax needs delivery. Executives need confidence. Regulators need compliance. Kira must navigate all of this.

---

#### Phase 2: Trust Breakdown Analysis (15 min)

**Prompt:** "The case centers on 'trust breakdown' between Kira and Dax. Let's understand how trust broke down and why Kira's attempts to fix it haven't worked."

**Guided Questions:**
- "How did trust break down between Kira and Dax?"
- "What are the signs that Dax doesn't trust Kira?"
- "Kira tried 'explaining the details' to Dax. Why didn't that work?"

**Create Analysis:**

**How Trust Broke:**
1. Commitments made (possibly without full engineering input)
2. First deadline missed
3. Explanation given, new commitment made
4. Second deadline missed
5. More explanations, less credibility
6. Pattern continues: miss → explain → miss → explain
7. Dax stops trusting explanations, starts escalating

**Why "Explaining Details" Doesn't Work:**
- Trust isn't rebuilt through words, but through actions
- Dax has heard explanations before, then seen more delays
- Explanations feel like excuses
- Dax needs confidence in path forward, not understanding of past

**Why Dax Keeps Escalating:**
- Believes "relaxing pressure could lead to further delays"
- May think pressure is only thing driving progress
- Doesn't trust the current path will succeed
- Faces own pressures from regulatory agencies
- Escalation is his way of seeking help or accountability

**Critical Question:**
> "What would rebuild trust with Dax? Not explanations—what would actually rebuild trust?"

**Answers to Draw Out:**
- Delivering something (anything) on time
- Being transparently realistic about what's possible
- Involving Dax in trade-off discussions
- Showing Dax the work and progress directly
- Making smaller commitments and keeping them
- Acknowledging past failures honestly

**Key Insight:** Trust is rebuilt through transparency and delivered commitments, not through explanations of why past commitments weren't met.

---

#### Phase 3: Root Cause Exploration (10-15 min)

**Prompt:** "The team is highly motivated but missing deadlines and approaching burnout. What's really causing this? Let's dig for root causes."

**Build Root Cause Tree Together:**

**Surface Symptom:** Missing deadlines

**Contributing Factors:**
- Work more complex than estimated?
- Dependencies not understood?
- Requirements changing?
- Technical blockers?
- Unrealistic commitments?
- Team capacity insufficient?

**Deeper Root Causes:**
- "Dependencies that were not fully understood when commitments were made"
- Who made commitments? With what input?
- Was there estimation process?
- Were engineers consulted?

**The Brooks's Law Problem:**
- Kira added engineers
- "Steep ramp-up time" means they slow the team down
- Why did adding people seem like solution?
- What does it tell us that it didn't work?

**The Burnout Signal:**
- Team "highly motivated" and "deeply committed"
- "Brink of burnout"
- This combination says: problem isn't motivation, it's systemic
- Can't work harder; must work differently

**Discussion Questions:**
- "If the team is already motivated and working hard, what lever does Kira actually have?"
- "What systemic issues need to be addressed?"

**Key Insight:** This isn't a motivation problem (team is motivated) or an effort problem (team is working hard). It's a systemic problem: commitments were made without full understanding, creating an impossible situation. Adding more effort or people won't fix it.

---

#### Phase 4: Failed Solutions Analysis (10 min)

**Prompt:** "Kira has tried two things that didn't work. Let's understand why they failed and what that teaches us."

**Solution 1: Adding Engineers**

**Why this seemed reasonable:**
- More people = more capacity (in theory)
- Common response to being behind

**Why it failed:**
- Steep ramp-up time for Helios work
- Existing team must train new people
- Brooks's Law: adding people to late project makes it later
- Doesn't address systemic issues

**What this teaches:**
- Can't just throw bodies at the problem
- Need to understand root cause first
- Some problems get worse with more people

**Solution 2: Explaining Details to Dax**

**Why this seemed reasonable:**
- If Dax understood complexity, he'd be more patient
- Technical explanation should create empathy

**Why it failed:**
- Trust already broken from missed commitments
- Explanations feel like excuses
- Doesn't give Dax what he needs (confidence)
- Dax has own pressures he's managing

**What this teaches:**
- Can't explain your way out of broken trust
- Must deliver results, not explanations
- Need to understand stakeholder's actual concerns and pressures

**Critical Question:**
> "If both of Kira's approaches failed, what should she try instead?"

---

#### Phase 5: Rebuilding Trust and Supporting Team (15 min)

**Prompt:** "So what should Kira and Riker actually do? Let's develop a multi-faceted approach."

**Build This Strategy Together:**

**Immediate Actions (This Week):**

**1. Create Honest Conversation with Dax**
- Acknowledge: "I know trust is broken. We've missed deadlines we committed to."
- Be transparent: "Here's what we understand now that we didn't when we committed."
- Involve Dax in trade-offs: "We can deliver X by [date], Y by [later date], or partial Z by [date]. What's most important to regulatory agencies?"
- Shift from defending to problem-solving together

**2. Reset Expectations with Executives**
- "Just fix it" isn't actionable guidance
- Kira should: "Here's the situation. Here are the options with trade-offs. Here's my recommendation. I need your support in [specific way]."
- Make trade-offs explicit: scope, quality, timeline—pick two

**3. Give Team Breathing Room**
- Communicate: "We're resetting expectations. You don't need to work nights and weekends."
- Reduce scope or defer other work
- Protect team from escalation pressure
- Show team their leadership is managing stakeholders

**Medium-term Actions (Next Month):**

**4. Deliver Something Small**
- Identify quick win that demonstrates progress
- Make specific, small commitment and deliver it on time
- Build credibility incrementally
- "We said we'd deliver X by Thursday. Here it is."

**5. Increase Transparency**
- Show Dax actual progress regularly (demos, sprint reviews)
- Make work visible, not just reported
- Let Dax see the team working, not just hear about it
- Builds empathy and understanding

**6. Address Root Causes**
- How were initial commitments made?
- What estimation process exists?
- Are engineers consulted before commitments?
- Fix this pattern for future projects

**Long-term Actions (Next Quarter):**

**7. Establish Sustainable Pace**
- After crisis, team needs recovery time
- Build buffer into future commitments
- Invest in tooling, automation, documentation
- Pay down technical debt

**8. Build Better Stakeholder Partnership**
- Regular Dax-Kira-Riker check-ins
- Involve Dax earlier in planning
- Educate Dax about technical realities
- Build relationship in non-crisis times

**9. Improve Commitment Process**
- Never commit without engineering input
- Include contingency in estimates
- Make assumptions explicit
- Have escalation path for when assumptions prove wrong

**The Pass/Fail Reframing:**

**Current Framing (Doesn't Work):**
- "Will Aurora be ready for regulatory review: yes or no?"
- Creates defensive, blame-oriented dynamic

**Better Framing:**
- "What audit capabilities can we demonstrate by [date]?"
- "What would make regulators most confident we're on the right path?"
- "If we phase this, what's the critical first phase?"
- Creates collaborative, problem-solving dynamic

**Discussion:**
> "Notice how reframing from pass/fail to spectrum opens up options. What other reframes might help?"

---

### Handling Difficult Moments

#### If Students Blame Dax for Unreasonable Escalation

**Intervention:**
> "Dax has made commitments to regulatory agencies. His credibility is on the line. He's tried trusting Kira's explanations and seen more delays. From his perspective, what choice does he have? How might you act in his situation?"

*Build empathy for Dax's position.*

#### If Students Think More Pressure Will Help

**Intervention:**
> "The team is already 'highly motivated' and at 'brink of burnout.' What happens if we add more pressure? Can you motivate people into working harder than they already are?"

*Surface that this is systemic, not motivational.*

#### If Students Suggest Just Meeting the Deadline

**Intervention:**
> "If it were possible to just meet the deadline, wouldn't they have done it? What constraints exist that make this hard? What would need to change to make it possible?"

*Push toward understanding real constraints.*

#### If Discussion Stays at Surface Level

**Intervention:**
> "We're talking about tactics. Let's go deeper: Why is Helios in this situation? What decisions created this crisis? How do we prevent it next time?"

## Key Teaching Points to Ensure Coverage

Make sure these concepts emerge during discussion:

### ✓ **Trust rebuilt through delivery, not explanation**
- Explanations don't rebuild broken trust
- Small delivered commitments do
- Transparency + results = trust

### ✓ **Escalation indicates communication failure**
- Stakeholder escalation is information
- Points to trust or communication breakdown
- Must understand stakeholder's real concerns

### ✓ **Burnout + missed deadlines = systemic problem**
- Not a motivation problem
- Not an effort problem
- Systemic issues need systemic solutions

### ✓ **Adding people to late projects backfires**
- Brooks's Law is real
- Ramp-up time slows existing team
- Not all problems solved by more people

### ✓ **Commitments without understanding create failure**
- Must involve engineering in commitments
- Need realistic estimation processes
- Assumptions must be explicit

### ✓ **Pass/fail framing prevents problem-solving**
- Binary thinking creates defensiveness
- Spectrum thinking creates collaboration
- Reframing opens options

### ✓ **Leaders must manage in multiple directions**
- Up: educate and set realistic expectations
- Down: protect team and provide clarity
- Across: partner with stakeholders
- Can't just transmit pressure

### ✓ **Sustainability requires breathing room**
- Can't build sustainable practices in crisis
- Must create relief to establish healthy patterns
- Burnout prevention needs real workload reduction

> "What's the most important thing you learned about managing stakeholder relationships and team health from this case?"

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### ❌ **Blaming Dax for Unreasonable Escalation**
He has legitimate pressures. Build empathy.

### ❌ **Thinking More Pressure Will Help**
Team already motivated and overworked. Won't help.

### ❌ **Accepting "Just Fix It" as Reasonable Direction**
Not actionable. Kira must make trade-offs explicit.

### ❌ **Missing the Brooks's Law Lesson**
Adding people to late projects often makes them later.

### ❌ **Not Addressing Root Cause**
Commitments made without full understanding is original problem.

### ❌ **Ignoring Burnout Implications**
Team sustainability is critical. Can't just push through.

## Assessment: What Good Discussion Looks Like

Students are learning well if they:

- ✅ Recognize trust is rebuilt through delivery, not explanation
- ✅ Empathize with all stakeholders' pressures
- ✅ Identify systemic issues beyond motivation or effort
- ✅ Understand why adding engineers didn't help
- ✅ See the value in reframing from pass/fail to spectrum
- ✅ Recognize the importance of team sustainability
- ✅ Develop multi-faceted approach (not single solution)
- ✅ Connect to their own experiences with stakeholder pressure

## Follow-Up Activities (Optional)

### Writing Exercise
Ask students to write:
1. The conversation Kira should have with Dax (300 words)
2. A policy for making external commitments (250 words)
3. Kira's message to the Helios team about path forward (200 words)

### Small Group Work
Divide into groups:
- Group A: You are Kira. Prepare for conversation with Dax.
- Group B: You are Dax. What do you need to hear from Kira?
- Group C: You are Riker. How do you support the team through this?

Then role-play the Kira-Dax conversation.

### Case Analysis
Ask students to write about a time when they experienced broken stakeholder trust. How was it handled? What worked? What didn't?
