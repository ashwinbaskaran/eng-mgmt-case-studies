# Worf Case Study: Coach Guide

## Key Management Learnings

### 1. Career Development Is an Ongoing Responsibility
**Core Learning:** Great managers proactively create development opportunities rather than reactively responding when someone asks for a promotion.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Worf is in reactive mode—Troi had to ask instead of having regular career conversations
- Career discussions should happen quarterly, not just during reviews
- Waiting for someone to ask means you've waited too long

**Key Insight:** Regular career conversations where Troi's interests and Worf's observations could have led to intentional planning, not improvisation.

### 2. Understanding Motivation Matters More Than Granting Requests
**Core Learning:** When someone asks for a specific role, understand the underlying motivation, not just the literal request.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Troi wants to "be an engineering manager"—but why?
- Different motivations (impact, autonomy, mentorship, recognition) suggest different solutions
- Ask "what about the role appeals to you?" before solving

**Key Insight:** Effective managers explore "why" before "how." The answer reveals whether the solution is a management title or something else entirely.

### 3. Constraints Often Breed Creativity
**Core Learning:** Limited resources force creative solutions that can be better than the obvious approach.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- "How many managers does a team of six need?" seems limiting
- But constraints force innovation: hybrid roles, new team structures, cross-team opportunities
- Sometimes the best solutions emerge from constraints

**Key Insight:** Worf can't just "make Troi a manager of 3 people" without inefficiency. This constraint might lead to better solutions: tech lead role, managing a new initiative, or growing into a role as the team expands.

### 4. Team Composition Changes What's Possible
**Core Learning:** The nature of the team—seniority, independence, work style—affects what structures will work.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Acme Apps is "senior, broadly knowledgeable, independent, intrinsically motivated"
- This team might not need intensive management
- Tech lead or player-coach models might work here where they wouldn't elsewhere

**Key Insight:** Solution should fit the team's actual needs, not just organizational convention.

### 5. Growth Planning Has Multiple Dimensions
**Core Learning:** Consider team growth, company growth, role evolution, and individual development timelines.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- If Acme Apps grows to 12 people, splitting makes sense
- If company is growing, other management opportunities may emerge
- Timing matters—what's available now vs. in 6 months

**Key Insight:** Think probabilistically: "If X happens, then Y becomes possible." Good managers actively shape growth opportunities.

### 6. Internal Mobility Benefits Organizations
**Core Learning:** Companies that enable internal career development retain talent, build institutional knowledge, and maintain culture.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Acme "values and encourages internal mobility and growth"
- Troi wants to stay—this loyalty is valuable
- Supporting internal moves often better than external hiring

**Key Insight:** Worf should leverage Acme's culture to ask: "What other teams need managers?" "Can we create a new role?" "How can leadership help?"

### 7. Acting Roles Create Expectations
**Core Learning:** When someone takes an interim role, there should be clarity about what it means for their future.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Troi "thrived" as acting manager—she proved herself
- Acting roles are auditions; success creates expectations
- Without explicit conversation, people assume success = promotion

**Key Insight:** Before his sabbatical, Worf could have framed expectations: "This is a development opportunity. If it goes well, we'll work together to find the right path."

### 8. The Empathy-Decision Balance
**Core Learning:** Managers must balance empathy for individuals with sound organizational decisions.

**What Students Should Understand:**
- Troi "earned the right to be supported"
- But creating inefficient structure helps no one long-term
- True support means finding the right opportunity, not just saying yes

**Key Insight:** Great managers hold the tension between supporting individuals and serving organizational needs.

## Facilitation Overview

### Session Structure (60-75 minutes)
1. Introduction (5 min)
2. Understanding Motivations (10-15 min)
3. Exploring Options (20 min)
4. Growth Scenarios (10-15 min)
5. Root Cause and Prevention (10 min)
6. Synthesis and Takeaways (10 min)

---

## Opening the Discussion

**Suggested Opening:**
"Worf faces a wonderful problem: a talented engineer who proved herself wants to grow into management. But his team is only 6 people. How do we honor Troi's development while maintaining organizational effectiveness?"

**Initial Poll:** Split the team / Find Troi another team / Create different role / Something else

---

## Discussion Flow

### Phase 1: Understanding Troi's Motivation (10-15 min)

**Prompt:** "Before we solve for Worf, let's understand Troi. What does she actually want?"

**Build This List:**

**Possible Motivations:**
- Impact: Shape team direction and strategy
- Mentorship: Coach and develop others
- Influence: Voice in organizational decisions
- Growth: Learn new skills
- Recognition: Title and status
- Autonomy: Freedom to make decisions

**Critical Question:** "Different motivations suggest different solutions. If Troi wants mentorship, she might not need to be THE manager. Why does this matter?"

**Answer to Draw Out:** You can't solve the problem until you understand what problem you're solving.

### Phase 2: Team Composition and Creative Options (15-20 min)

**Prompt:** "The team is 'senior, broadly knowledgeable, independent, intrinsically motivated.' How does this affect what's possible?"

**Brainstorm Options:**

**Restructure Current Team:**
- Split into two 3-person teams
- Tech lead role for Troi with management elements
- Worf moves to managing managers

**Create New Opportunity:**
- Troi manages new initiative within Apps
- Troi manages cross-team function
- Start new team for emerging need

**Find Existing Opportunity:**
- Another team needs manager
- Adjacent team where manager is leaving

**Hybrid Approaches:**
- "Manager in training" with gradual responsibility
- 6-month plan: if team grows, split then

**For Each Option:** "What would make this work? What are the risks? How does this serve both Troi and the business?"

### Phase 3: Growth Scenarios (10-15 min)

**Prompt:** "What if the team grows? What if the company grows?"

**Build Matrix:**

| Scenario | Implication | What Worf Can Do |
|----------|-------------|------------------|
| Apps grows to 12+ | Splitting makes sense | Accelerate hiring, prepare split |
| Apps won't grow | Need different solution | Create role or find other team |
| Company growing | Other teams need managers | Network Troi, explore options |

**Critical Question:** "Can Worf *influence* any of these? How?"

### Phase 4: What Worf Could Have Done Earlier (10 min)

**Prompt:** "What could Worf have done earlier?"

**Build This List:**

**Before Sabbatical:**
- Discuss what acting role means
- Set expectations about what happens when he returns
- Make commitments about career conversations

**During Regular 1:1s:**
- Understand Troi's career aspirations
- Create development plan
- Look for opportunities proactively

**Key Learning:** "These are all proactive career development practices. What would your work be like if your manager did these?"

### Phase 5: The Conversation (10 min)

**Exercise:** "You're Worf. Troi just said she wants to be an EM. What do you say?"

**Good Response Elements:**
- Acknowledge her success and contribution
- Express genuine commitment to her development
- Ask questions to understand motivation
- Be honest about constraints
- Commit to working together on finding the path

**Example:** "You did an amazing job while I was away. I want to support your development—you've earned that. Help me understand what you enjoyed most and what you're hoping for. There may not be an immediate path to managing half this team, but I'm committed to finding the right opportunity, whether here or elsewhere at Acme."

---

## Key Teaching Points

✓ Career development is proactive, not reactive
✓ Understand motivation before solving
✓ Team composition affects structure
✓ Constraints breed creativity
✓ Growth scenarios change options
✓ Internal mobility is strategic

---

## Handling Difficult Moments

**If Students Jump to "Split the Team":**
"Let's pressure-test that. Would two 3-person teams have enough work? Is this efficient?"

**If Students Suggest Troi Should Leave:**
"Troi wants to stay at Acme. What does that tell us about internal mobility as organizational strength?"

**If Discussion Avoids Hard Trade-offs:**
"Supporting Troi might mean honest conversation that the role isn't immediately available. Is that okay?"

---

## Debrief

**Personal Application:** "How often do you have career conversations? What will you do differently?"

**Key Takeaways:** "What's most important about career development from this case?"

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Jumping to solutions without understanding motivation
- Accepting "split the team" without analysis
- Treating organizational constraints as fixed
- Ignoring the other 5 team members
- Forgetting Worf could have been proactive earlier
- Viewing empathy and hard decisions as opposites

---

*Remember: Your role as facilitator is to guide discovery, not to provide "the answer." The best learning comes from students wrestling with complexity and articulating their own insights.*
