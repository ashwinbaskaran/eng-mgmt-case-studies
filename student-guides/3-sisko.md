# Sisko Case Study: Student Guide

## Purpose
This guide will help you prepare for a productive discussion of the Sisko case study. The goal is to develop your thinking about team scaling patterns, delegation strategies, and how a manager's role evolves as teams grow.

## How to Use This Guide

1. **Read the case study thoroughly** (3-sisko.md)
2. **Work through the reflection questions** below

4. **Come prepared** to discuss your perspectives and listen to others

---

## Case Study Overview

**Key Players:**
- **Sisko**: Engineering manager of the Banks team, currently also serves as lead engineer
- **The Banks Team**: Currently 6 people, approved to grow to 15
- **Leadership**: Giving Sisko wide latitude in shaping the growth

**The Situation:**
Sisko must present an annual plan that includes growing the team from 6 to 15 people. Having been focused on building the first integrations as a player-coach, Sisko hasn't thought about how his role might change with growth. He needs to understand growth patterns and their implications.

---

## Pre-Discussion Reflection Questions

### Understanding the Current State

1. **What is Sisko's current role?**
   - Engineering manager + lead engineer ("doubling as lead engineer")
   - What does this dual role tell us about the team's history?
   - What are the advantages and disadvantages of this model?

2. **What does the team's work involve?**
   - Bespoke integrations with central bank infrastructures
   - High ongoing maintenance
   - No standards (every integration is unique)
   - What skills and structure does this type of work require?

3. **Why is the team growing?**
   - Success with initial banks (New Zealand, Japan, Barbados)
   - Larger, more complex integrations ahead
   - More parallel new integrations needed
   - Ongoing maintenance of existing integrations
   - What does this tell us about future work?

### Understanding Growth Implications

4. **What happens when a team more than doubles in size?**
   - Communication complexity
   - Coordination overhead
   - Management attention per person
   - Culture and team dynamics

5. **How might Sisko's role need to change?**
   - From player-coach to full-time manager?
   - From individual contributor to delegator?
   - From doing to enabling?
   - What skills might Sisko need to develop?

6. **What are different ways to structure a 15-person team?**
   - One large team?
   - Multiple smaller teams?
   - Organized by function? By customer? By technology?
   - What are the tradeoffs of each?

## Inflection Points

Research suggests manager roles change at certain team sizes. Consider:

**1-6 people:**
- Manager can know everything happening
- Direct communication with everyone
- Player-coach model viable

**7-12 people:**
- Manager can't track all details
- Need more structure and process
- Delegation becomes essential

**12+ people:**
- Manager of managers often emerges
- Formal team structure required
- Manager focuses on strategy, not tactics
