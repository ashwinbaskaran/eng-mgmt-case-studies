# Sisko Case Study: Student Guide

## Overview

Sisko manages the Banks team, which builds bespoke integrations with central bank infrastructures. Currently 6 people with Sisko acting as both manager and lead engineer, the team is approved to grow to 15. Sisko must present an annual plan for this growth but hasn't deeply considered how the role needs to evolve from player-coach to full-time manager.

## Preparing for Discussion

Consider these questions as you think about the case:

**Understanding the Current State**

Sisko is "doubling as lead engineer" while managing. What does this dual role tell you about the team's history and stage? What are the advantages and limitations of this model?

The work involves bespoke central bank integrations with high ongoing maintenance and no standards. What does this tell you about the skills, autonomy, and structure the team needs?

The team is growing because of success with New Zealand, Japan, and Barbados, with larger integrations and more parallel work ahead. What does this suggest about future complexity?

**The Challenge of Scale**

What happens when a team more than doubles in size? Think about communication complexity, coordination overhead, how much attention each person gets, and cultural dynamics.

Can Sisko continue as lead engineer while managing 15 people? What would need to stop? What would need to start? At what team size does the player-coach model typically break?

**Structural Options**

What are the natural ways to subdivide this work? By customer? By technology? By function? What are the tradeoffs of each approach?

Consider different team structures: one flat 15-person team, sub-teams with tech leads, hierarchical teams with a manager-of-managers, or pod/squad models organized around customers. When does each work well? When does each struggle?

How many people should report directly to Sisko? What's a reasonable span of control for this type of work?

**Role Evolution**

Think about how Sisko's role needs to evolve: from doing to enabling, from individual contributor to delegator, from player-coach to full-time manager. What new skills might Sisko need to develop?

What are the signs that a manager is stretched too thin? What should Sisko be watching for?

**Delegation and Leadership Development**

What responsibilities could Sisko delegate as the team grows? Who on the current team might take on leadership roles?

How do you develop leaders within a team? What experiences and support do emerging leaders need?

**Inflection Points**

Manager roles typically change at certain team sizes. With 1-6 people, you can know everything happening and player-coach models work. At 7-12 people, you can't track all details and delegation becomes essential. At 12+ people, formal structure is often needed and managers focus on strategy over tactics.

Where will Sisko face the biggest challenges as the team grows? What transitions will be hardest?

## Questions to Explore

What should Sisko's time allocation look like at 6 people versus 15? What must change in how decisions get made? How communication flows? How hiring and onboarding work?

What support should Sisko ask from Acme leadership? What resources, training, or coaching might help? What organizational systems need to be in place?

How does the bespoke nature of the work affect the options? Does every integration being unique make it harder or easier to subdivide the team?

## Personal Reflection

Have you been part of a team that grew significantly? What changed? What stayed the same?

What's the difference between being a tech lead and a people manager? Have you seen both roles done well?

If you were Sisko, what would you be most excited about with this growth? What would worry you most?

## For Discussion

Think about: What should Sisko's annual plan include? How should the team be structured? What does Sisko need to learn or change? How can organizations better support managers through rapid team growth?

Remember: There's no single "right answer." The goal is to develop your judgment and learn from exploring different perspectives together.
