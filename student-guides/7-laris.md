# Laris Case Study: Student Guide

## Overview

Laris is the new engineering manager for Starfleet, a critical project that could establish an insurmountable competitive lead. Agnes, a "roving lead engineer" who moves between crisis projects, joined Starfleet two weeks ago and proposed a modular approach requiring a new sub-team. After 8 weeks and 5 candidates with "very rigorous" screening, no hire has been made—the hiring committee (Agnes, Elnor, and Jack) hasn't given unanimous approval to anyone. Leadership is desperate to see Starfleet shipped. Laris needs to figure out what's going on.

## Preparing for Discussion

Consider these questions as you think about the case:

**Understanding Agnes and the Roving Engineer Pattern**

Agnes is a "roving lead engineer" who moves between crisis projects and has helped many teams with difficult challenges. She's worked with both Elnor and Jack before. What does this pattern tell you? What are the benefits of having roving engineers? What are the costs or risks?

When Agnes joined Starfleet, she took 2 weeks to "gain her bearings," recommended a modular approach, and asked to create a new sub-team. Leadership "eagerly agreed." What does this tell you about Agnes's influence? About leadership's approach to Agnes's requests?

Put yourself in Agnes's shoes. Brought in to fix another crisis, sees a solution, needs a team to execute it, but can't hire anyone after 8 weeks. What might Agnes be feeling?

**Understanding Starfleet's Strategic Importance**

Starfleet "could easily establish an almost insurmountable lead against competition." Growth is "starting to flatten out in other areas" with "few other substantial ideas." The company is "desperate to see Starfleet shipped." What pressure does this create? How might this desperation affect decision-making?

**Understanding the Hiring Problem**

After 8 weeks and 5 candidates with "very rigorous" screening, the hiring committee hasn't given unanimous approval to anyone. What might "very rigorous" mean? When does rigor become an obstacle?

Why might there be no unanimous approval? Consider: different standards, different assessment approaches, different priorities for the role, interpersonal dynamics, strategic disagreements. What else might explain it?

What does "unanimous approval" mean? Why is it required? Is this typical? What are the tradeoffs of requiring unanimity?

What are the costs of 8 weeks without a hire? Think about time lost, Starfleet still not progressing, opportunity cost, candidate experience, team morale.

**Understanding the Stakeholders**

Put yourself in Elnor's shoes—a founding Starfleet member who worked with Agnes before on scaling. What concerns might Elnor have about Agnes's approach? About bringing in a new lead engineer?

Put yourself in Jack's shoes—a founding Starfleet member who worked with Agnes before and referred Laris for the manager role. What might Jack be thinking about the hiring process? About Agnes's proposal?

Put yourself in Laris's shoes—new to the team and company, referred by Jack, team is stuck and can't hire, project is critical. What challenges does Laris face? What credibility does Laris have? What doesn't Laris know yet?

**Forming Hypotheses**

Could this be a hiring bar calibration problem? Different people with different standards, Agnes looking for the perfect candidate, unrealistic expectations, comparing candidates to themselves?

Could this be a role clarity problem? Unclear what the role actually needs, modular approach not well-defined, different people wanting different things?

Could this be about power dynamics and trust? Elnor and Jack don't trust Agnes's approach, passive resistance through the hiring process, disagreement about technical strategy masked as hiring concerns?

Could this be about the roving engineer pattern itself? Agnes parachutes in, recommends changes, needs new team—pattern creates resentment, existing team feels undermined, leadership too quick to give Agnes what she asks for?

**Teasing Apart the Decisions**

Map the key decisions and their consequences: Moving Agnes to Starfleet. Agnes recommending the modular approach. Leadership creating a new sub-team and budget. Agnes leading the hiring process. For each: What were the tradeoffs? What was the rationale? What were the consequences?

**People, Technical, and Process Issues**

The case asks you to consider issues along different axes. What people-related problems might be at play? What technical issues? What process problems? How do these interact?

**What Should Laris Investigate?**

What does Laris need to understand about the hiring process? About the technical strategy? About team dynamics? About the roving engineer pattern at Acme?

What questions should Laris ask Agnes? Elnor? Jack? Leadership? What should Laris observe?

## Questions to Explore

How might each stakeholder be contributing to the problem? How might each contribute to a solution?

What would happen if Laris lowered the hiring bar? What would happen if the hiring process continues unchanged?

Is the modular approach the right technical strategy? How can Laris assess that without deep technical context?

What precedent does the roving engineer pattern set? What incentives does it create?

## Personal Reflection

Have you been on a team where hiring took forever? What was really going on beneath the surface?

Have you worked with "parachute" consultants or roving experts? How did it feel? What worked? What didn't?

If you were Laris, where would you start investigating? Who would you talk to first?

## For Discussion

Think about: What should Laris do now? What's really causing the hiring gridlock? How should organizations use roving engineers effectively? What decision-making patterns led to this situation?

Remember: There's no single "right answer." The goal is to develop your judgment and learn from exploring different perspectives together.
