# Sulu Case Study: Student Guide

## Overview

Sulu manages the Carboni team—highly specialized engineers working at the intersection of cryptocurrency, economics, and clean tech. A major government opportunity requires specialized work in 9 months. Three months have passed with no hire, leaving 6 months to deadline. Should Sulu hire quickly and train, or continue searching for the perfect specialized candidate?

## Preparing for Discussion

Consider these questions as you think about the case:

**Understanding the Constraints**

What are the real constraints Sulu faces? Consider time, skills needed, team capacity, and business impact. Which of these constraints might be negotiable? Which are truly fixed?

When Sulu assumes it will take 3 months to train someone or 3 months for an experienced engineer to execute, what might be missing from those estimates? What could go wrong?

**Doing the Math**

Walk through the timeline for each option. If Sulu hires a generalist today, how much training time is needed? How much time remains for actual work? Include onboarding, ramp-up, and execution.

If Sulu keeps searching for a specialist, how long might that realistically take, given the last three months? How much time remains for execution? Where's the buffer for things going wrong?

**Beyond the Binary**

If neither "hire quickly" nor "keep searching" seems likely to succeed, what does that tell you? What other approaches might exist?

Consider: Could the work be scoped differently? Could other team members contribute? Could external resources help? Could the timeline be negotiated?

**What Happened in Those Three Months?**

Sulu has been searching for three months without success. What should Sulu have been doing during that time? At what point should Sulu have escalated or changed approach?

Think about the difference between passive hiring (post and wait) and active hiring (multiple parallel strategies). What would active hiring look like here?

**Communication and Stakeholder Management**

Leadership likely doesn't know Sulu has been unable to hire for 3 months. What should Sulu communicate up the management chain? When? How?

What's the difference between "I'm working on it" and effective risk communication?

**The Bigger Picture**

The Carboni team has "hard commitments including features needed to hit revenue targets for an upcoming IPO." How does this new project affect those commitments?

What if the right answer is "we can't do both well"? How would you make that case?

## Questions to Explore

What pressures is Sulu experiencing? What information might Sulu not have? What responsibilities does Sulu have to different stakeholders?

If you were on the Carboni team, how would this new hire impact you? What support might the team need if someone less-experienced joins?

From leadership's perspective, what do they care about most? What information do they need from Sulu? How might they be able to help?

## Personal Reflection

Have you experienced hiring pressure before? What happened?

How do you typically handle situations where you're behind on a critical deadline?

What would "success" look like in this situation, given the constraints?

## For Discussion

Think about: What should Sulu do now? What should Sulu have done differently in the first three months? How can managers avoid falling into this trap?

Remember: There's no single "right answer." The goal is to develop your judgment and learn from exploring different perspectives together.
