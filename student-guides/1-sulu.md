# Sulu Case Study: Student Guide

## Purpose
This guide will help you prepare for a productive discussion of the Sulu case study. The goal is to develop your thinking about hiring under pressure, deadline management, and stakeholder communication in engineering organizations.

## How to Use This Guide

1. **Read the case study thoroughly** (1-sulu.md)
2. **Work through the reflection questions** below
3. **Complete the decision framework** to organize your thinking
4. **Come prepared** to discuss your perspectives and listen to others

---

## Case Study Overview

**Key Players:**
- **Sulu**: Manager of the Carboni team at Acme Corp
- **The Carboni Team**: Highly specialized engineers working at the intersection of cryptocurrency, economics, and clean tech
- **Leadership/Stakeholders**: Expecting delivery for a major government initiative

**The Situation:**
A major government opportunity requires specialized work to be delivered in 9 months. Three months have passed with no hire, leaving 6 months to deadline. Sulu must decide whether to hire quickly and train, or continue searching for the perfect specialized candidate.

---

## Pre-Discussion Reflection Questions

### Understanding the Context

1. **What are the real constraints Sulu is facing?**
   - Time constraints
   - Skill requirements
   - Team capacity
   - Business impact

2. **What assumptions is Sulu making?**
   - About training timelines (3 months)
   - About execution speed (3 months for experienced engineer)
   - About the hiring market
   - About what's negotiable

### Understanding the Trade-offs

3. **What are the risks of each hiring approach?**
   - Hiring quickly (generalist who needs training)
   - Continuing to search (specialist with domain expertise)
   - Consider: What if neither approach succeeds in time?

4. **Beyond the binary choice, what else could be done?**
   - Could the work be scoped differently?
   - Could other team members contribute?
   - Could external resources help?
   - Could the timeline be negotiated?

### Understanding Stakeholder Perspectives

5. **Put yourself in Sulu's shoes:**
   - What pressures is Sulu experiencing?
   - What information does Sulu need but might not have?
   - What are Sulu's responsibilities to different stakeholders?

6. **Consider the existing team's perspective:**
   - How does this new hire impact them?
   - What support might they need if a less-experienced person joins?
   - What are the opportunity costs of training vs. executing?

7. **Consider leadership's perspective:**
   - What do they care about most?
   - What information do they need from Sulu?
   - How might they be able to help?
