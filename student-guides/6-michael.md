# Michael Case Study: Student Guide

## Purpose
This guide will help you prepare for a productive discussion of the Michael case study. The goal is to develop your thinking about productive conflict, decision-making effectiveness, and the hidden risks of high morale without progress.

## How to Use This Guide

1. **Read the case study thoroughly** (6-michael.md)
2. **Work through the reflection questions** below
3. **Complete the decision framework** to organize your thinking
4. **Come prepared** to discuss your perspectives and listen to others

---

## Case Study Overview

**Key Players:**
- **Michael**: New manager (6 months) of the backoffice engineering team
- **The Backoffice Team**: Senior, well-regarded engineers with strong opinions, collegial environment
- **Previous Manager**: Left on good terms; team was successful under their leadership
- **PeopleOps**: Interviewed team, found high morale despite lack of progress

**The Situation:**
A senior, hard-working team with high morale is falling behind on a key project despite being highly motivated. Michael has excellent 360 feedback and strong people skills. What's causing low productivity despite high effort and morale?

---

## Pre-Discussion Reflection Questions

### Understanding the Puzzle

1. **How can a team have high morale but fall behind?**
   - What's the difference between morale and productivity?
   - What's the difference between effort and results?
   - What might explain working hard without making progress?

2. **What could cause low productivity despite high effort?**
   - Technical blockers
   - Unclear requirements or scope
   - Poor decision-making processes
   - Wrong approach or architecture
   - External dependencies
   - Inefficient processes

3. **The case notes the team is "senior" with "strong opinions" in a "collegial environment." What might this tell us?**
   - How do strong opinions and collegiality interact?
   - Could being collegial create problems?
   - What does "collegial" mean for decision-making?

### Understanding Michael's Context

4. **Michael is new (6 months) to a team with a successful history. How might this affect dynamics?**
   - What credibility challenges might Michael face?
   - How might the team compare Michael to the previous manager?
   - What might Michael be hesitant to do?

5. **Michael has excellent 360 feedback. What does this tell us?**
   - What strengths does Michael have?
   - Could any of these strengths become weaknesses?
   - What might Michael be optimizing for?

6. **The case mentions "few, if any, conflicts on serious issues" at Acme. Is this good or bad?**
   - When is lack of conflict positive?
   - When is lack of conflict a warning sign?
   - What might it indicate about decision-making?

### Exploring Possible Root Causes

7. **What are different types of productivity problems?**
   - Technical (wrong architecture, technical debt, complexity)
   - Process (inefficient workflows, too many meetings, unclear responsibilities)
   - Decision-making (slow decisions, revisiting decisions, no decisions)
   - People (skill gaps, motivation, collaboration issues)
   - External (dependencies, changing requirements, stakeholder issues)

8. **Which of these could coexist with high morale and high effort?**
   - Think about which problems would make people unhappy vs. just unproductive
