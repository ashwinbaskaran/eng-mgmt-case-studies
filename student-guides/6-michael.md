# Michael Case Study: Student Guide

## Overview

Michael has managed the backoffice engineering team for 6 months. The team is senior, hard-working, collegial, with strong opinions. They have high morale and are highly motivated—but they're falling behind on migrating payment providers, a key project. Michael has excellent 360 feedback. PeopleOps interviewed the team and confirmed: high morale, low progress. What's causing low productivity despite high effort?

## Preparing for Discussion

Consider these questions as you think about the case:

**Understanding the Puzzle**

How can a team have high morale but fall behind? What's the difference between morale and productivity? Between effort and results?

What could cause low productivity despite high effort? Think about technical blockers, unclear requirements, poor decision-making processes, wrong approaches, external dependencies, inefficient processes. Which of these could coexist with high morale?

The team is "senior" with "strong opinions" in a "collegial environment." How do strong opinions and collegiality interact? Could being too collegial create problems? What does "collegial" mean for decision-making?

**Understanding Michael's Context**

Michael is new (6 months) to a team with a successful history. How might this affect dynamics? What credibility challenges might Michael face? What might Michael be hesitant to do?

Michael has excellent 360 feedback, including understanding employees' career objectives, willingness to advocate for them, and eagerness to give credit. What strengths does this reveal? Could any of these strengths become weaknesses? What might Michael be optimizing for? What might Michael be avoiding to maintain these positive perceptions?

The case notes there are "few, if any, conflicts on serious issues" at Acme. Is this good or bad? When is lack of conflict positive? When is it a warning sign? What might it indicate about decision-making?

**Exploring Possible Root Causes**

Could this be a technical problem? Underestimated complexity, technical debt, wrong architecture, integration challenges? What evidence would support or contradict this?

Could this be a decision-making problem? Decisions taking too long, too much consensus-seeking, decisions getting revisited, no clear decision-maker, "strong opinions" creating gridlock? What would that look like?

Could this be about lack of productive conflict? What if "collegial" means avoiding disagreement? Issues don't get debated thoroughly, people defer to avoid conflict, false consensus where everyone "agrees" but doesn't commit? How can you tell the difference between healthy agreement and false harmony?

Could this be about unclear requirements or changing scope? Scope creep, stakeholder misalignment, requirements changing, building the wrong thing?

**The Conflict Paradox**

When is lack of conflict positive? When is it negative? What's the difference between productive conflict and destructive conflict?

How do you encourage productive disagreement without creating destructive tension? What does healthy debate look like?

**Diagnostic Questions**

What questions should Michael ask the team? What should Michael ask stakeholders? What should Michael observe or investigate?

How can Michael diagnose the problem without making the team defensive? What would make people open up versus shut down?

**Considering Solutions**

What if Michael added more engineers? Would that solve the problem? What are the pros and cons?

What if Michael exchanged engineers with other teams? Would fresh perspectives help or would it disrupt team dynamics?

What else could Michael try? What would address decision-making issues? What would address conflict avoidance? What would address unclear requirements?

## Questions to Explore

Are there ways in which strong 360 feedback could be misleading? What might great interpersonal feedback hide?

What's the relationship between Michael's excellent 360 feedback and the team's low productivity? Could they be connected?

How might the previous manager's approach have contributed to current patterns? What expectations did that set?

## Personal Reflection

Have you been on a team that was working hard but not making progress? What was causing it? How did it feel?

What's your relationship with conflict? Do you avoid it or engage with it? How does that affect your effectiveness?

If you were Michael, what would you be most worried about? What would you want to know?

## For Discussion

Think about: What should Michael do to diagnose the root cause? What might Michael need to change about their own approach? How can teams maintain high morale while also having productive conflict and making progress?

Remember: There's no single "right answer." The goal is to develop your judgment and learn from exploring different perspectives together.
