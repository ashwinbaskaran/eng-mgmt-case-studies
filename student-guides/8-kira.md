# Kira Case Study: Student Guide

## Purpose
This guide will help you prepare for a productive discussion of the Kira case study. The goal is to develop your thinking about stakeholder trust management, burnout prevention, expectation management, and navigating escalation dynamics in engineering organizations.

## How to Use This Guide

1. **Read the case study thoroughly** (8-kira.md)
2. **Work through the reflection questions** below
3. **Complete the decision framework** to organize your thinking
4. **Come prepared** to discuss your perspectives and listen to others

---

## Case Study Overview

**Key Players:**
- **Kira**: Director of Engineering for Helios team
- **Riker**: Manager of the Helios engineering team
- **Dax**: Head of Government Relations, making commitments to regulatory agencies
- **The Helios Team**: Owns links to government treasuries, working on Aurora (audit platform)
- **Executive Team**: Asked Kira to "just fix it"

**The Situation:**
Helios has missed several key deadlines for Aurora, a critical compliance platform. The team is highly motivated but at the brink of burnout. Dax escalates to executives, flagging Aurora as a problem project. Kira tried adding engineers (didn't help due to ramp-up time) and explaining details to Dax (who continues escalating). Trust is broken, and Kira must rebuild it while supporting the team.

## Pre-Discussion Reflection Questions

### Understanding the Context

1. **What makes the Helios team's work challenging?**
   - "Deep technical expertise" required
   - "Long ramp-up period for new engineers"
   - Links to government treasuries
   - Compliance and regulatory requirements
   - What does this tell us about the work?

2. **What is Aurora and why does it matter?**
   - Audit platform for compliance with regulatory agencies
   - Dax has made commitments to regulators
   - "Stakes are high"
   - Potential consequences: "reputational damage, regulatory penalties, loss of key partnerships"
   - What pressure does this create?

3. **What's happened so far?**
   - Several missed deadlines
   - Challenges: integrating new tools, dependencies not fully understood when commitments made
   - Team is motivated but approaching burnout
   - Dax escalated to executives
   - Kira tried: adding engineers, explaining to Dax
   - Neither approach worked
   - What does this tell us?

### Understanding the Players

4. **Put yourself in Riker's shoes (team manager):**
   - Managing highly motivated but burned-out team
   - Missing deadlines despite team effort
   - Boss (Kira) is under pressure from executives
   - What might Riker be feeling?

5. **Put yourself in Kira's shoes (Director):**
   - Responsible for delivery to executives
   - Team is struggling and burning out
   - Stakeholder (Dax) doesn't trust her and keeps escalating
   - Tried two approaches, neither worked
   - Executives told her to "just fix it"
   - What pressures is Kira experiencing?

6. **Put yourself in Dax's shoes (Government Relations):**
   - Made commitments to regulatory agencies
   - Can't deliver on those commitments
   - Believes "relaxing pressure could lead to further delays"
   - Continues to escalate
   - What might Dax be worried about?

7. **Put yourself in the Helios team's shoes:**
   - Working extremely hard
   - Still missing deadlines
   - Approaching burnout
   - Stakeholder keeps escalating
   - What does the team need?

### Understanding the Dynamics

8. **Why didn't adding engineers help?**
   - "Steep ramp-up time"
   - What does this tell us about the solution approach?

9. **Why does Dax keep escalating despite Kira's explanations?**
   - "Dax feels relaxing pressure could lead to further delays"
   - What does this tell us about trust?

10. **What does "just fix it" from executives mean?**
    - Is this helpful guidance?
    - What does it tell us about executive understanding?
