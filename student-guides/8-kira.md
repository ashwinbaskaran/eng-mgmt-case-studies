# Kira Case Study: Student Guide

## Overview

Kira directs the Helios engineering team, which owns links to government treasuries and is building Aurora, a critical audit platform for regulatory compliance. Helios has missed several deadlines. The team is highly motivated but approaching burnout. Dax, Head of Government Relations, made commitments to regulators and keeps escalating to executives. Kira tried adding engineers—didn't help due to steep ramp-up time. Kira tried explaining details to Dax—Dax continues escalating, believing "relaxing pressure could lead to further delays." Trust is broken. Executives told Kira to "just fix it."

## Preparing for Discussion

Consider these questions as you think about the case:

**Understanding the Work and Its Challenges**

The Helios team's work requires "deep technical expertise" with "long ramp-up period for new engineers." They're integrating new tools while maintaining links to government treasuries with compliance requirements. What does this tell you about the work's complexity?

Aurora is an audit platform for regulatory agencies. Dax has made commitments to regulators. Potential consequences include "reputational damage, regulatory penalties, loss of key partnerships." What pressure does this create? How does working with regulators differ from typical product development?

**Understanding What's Happened**

Helios missed several deadlines. Challenges include integrating new tools and dependencies that weren't fully understood when commitments were made. Team is motivated but approaching burnout. Work backward: Why weren't dependencies understood? Why were commitments made without full information? What should have happened differently?

Kira tried adding engineers—didn't help due to steep ramp-up time. Why did this seem reasonable? Why didn't it work? What does this tell us about the nature of the problem?

Kira tried explaining details to Dax—Dax continues escalating. Why did this seem reasonable? Why didn't it work? What does this tell us about trust and communication?

**Understanding Each Person's Perspective**

Put yourself in the Helios team's shoes. Working extremely hard, still missing deadlines, approaching burnout, stakeholder keeps escalating. What does the team need? What would make things better versus worse?

Put yourself in Riker's shoes (team manager). Managing a highly motivated but burned-out team, missing deadlines despite effort, boss is under pressure from executives. What might Riker be feeling? What support does Riker need?

Put yourself in Kira's shoes (Director). Responsible for delivery to executives, team struggling and burning out, stakeholder doesn't trust you and keeps escalating, tried two approaches and neither worked, executives told you to "just fix it." What pressures are you experiencing? What options do you see?

Put yourself in Dax's shoes (Government Relations). Made commitments to regulatory agencies, can't deliver on those commitments, believe relaxing pressure could lead to further delays. What might Dax be worried about? Why does Dax keep escalating?

**Understanding the Trust Breakdown**

How did trust break down between Kira and Dax? What are the signs of broken trust? What makes it hard to rebuild trust in this situation?

Why does Dax believe "relaxing pressure could lead to further delays"? Is this belief reasonable? What would change Dax's mind?

What does "just fix it" from executives mean? Is this helpful guidance? What does it tell you about executive understanding of the situation?

**Thinking About Solutions**

What needs to happen in the short-term (next 2-4 weeks) to address the immediate crisis? What about medium-term (2-3 months) to rebuild trust and sustainable pace? What about long-term to prevent future crises?

What are the tradeoffs of continuing to add engineers versus optimizing the current team's workflow? What other options exist beyond these two?

How can Kira navigate stakeholder trust issues with Dax while ensuring the team has space and support to succeed? Must these be sequential or can they be parallel?

How might Kira and Riker engage Dax and other stakeholders to manage expectations without framing the project as pass/fail?

**Thinking About Expectation Management**

How could expectations have been managed differently when commitments were made? When challenges emerged? When deadlines were missed? What about now?

What's the difference between communicating status updates ("I'm working on it") and effective risk communication? What does Dax need to hear?

**Thinking About Sustainability**

What actions should Kira and Riker prioritize to ensure long-term sustainability for Helios while addressing the immediate crisis?

What are the signs that a team is approaching burnout? What can be done to prevent it? What can be done once it's already happening?

How do you balance the urgency of regulatory requirements with team sustainability?

## Questions to Explore

What are the key factors contributing to the team's struggles? Are they primarily technical, organizational, or interpersonal?

If you could change one decision that was made earlier, which would have the biggest impact?

What role should executives play in this situation? What support could they provide?

What would success look like 6 months from now? For the team? For Kira? For Dax? For the company?

## Personal Reflection

Have you been on a team that was overcommitted and burning out? What helped? What made it worse?

Have you experienced a stakeholder who kept escalating despite explanations? What was that like? What worked?

If you were Kira, what would you do first? What would you be most worried about?

## For Discussion

Think about: What should Kira do now? How can trust be rebuilt with Dax? How can the team be supported while still delivering? What systemic changes would prevent this situation in the future?

Remember: There's no single "right answer." The goal is to develop your judgment and learn from exploring different perspectives together.
