# Context

Acme Corp's Infrastructure team, led by Geordi, is responsible for the core systems that power CarboniChain - the distributed ledger technology at the heart of Acme's decarbonization platform. The team of eight engineers maintains the consensus protocol, node infrastructure, and the critical real-time monitoring systems that banks and governments depend on for 24/7 uptime.

Geordi has been with Acme for three years and built much of the Infrastructure team from the ground up. He prides himself on creating an environment where engineers have autonomy and are trusted to own their domains. The team has consistently delivered high-quality work, and Geordi has been recognized as one of Acme's strongest engineering managers.

Julian is a senior engineer who joined the Infrastructure team eighteen months ago with impressive credentials - he previously built consensus systems at a well-known blockchain company. During his first year, Julian made substantial contributions to the node infrastructure, earning praise from teammates and stakeholders. However, over the last six months, Geordi has noticed a concerning pattern. Julian's code reviews have become sporadic and often miss critical issues. His designs for new features have required significant rework. Most troubling, a production incident three weeks ago was traced back to a configuration change Julian made without following the standard review process - something completely uncharacteristic of someone at his level.

When Geordi initially brought up these concerns in their 1:1s, Julian acknowledged the issues but attributed them to being "stretched thin" across multiple priorities. Geordi adjusted Julian's workload, removing him from two concurrent projects. Yet the problems have persisted. In fact, Julian has missed several deadlines on his now-singular focus area - upgrading the monitoring system to support new regulatory reporting requirements.

The situation is complicated by several factors. Julian remains well-liked by the team and is often the person others turn to for historical context about infrastructure decisions. He still participates actively in design discussions and his technical insights remain valuable. Additionally, two other engineers on the team privately mentioned to Geordi that they're concerned about Julian but aren't sure what's wrong - he seems disengaged in team meetings but insists everything is fine. Geordi has checked in with PeopleOps and confirmed there are no reported personal issues, though he knows such things aren't always visible.

# Challenge

Geordi needs to address Julian's performance issues, but he's uncertain about the root cause and the right approach. Is this a temporary rough patch, a sign of misalignment between Julian's strengths and current work, a motivation issue, something personal that Julian hasn't shared, or something else entirely? How should Geordi approach this situation to both support Julian and protect the team's effectiveness and the critical systems they maintain?

# Discussion Prompts

- What additional information would help Geordi better understand what's happening with Julian? How might he gather this information?
- How might Julian's previous strong performance influence how Geordi should approach this situation differently than if Julian had been consistently underperforming?
- What are the risks of acting too quickly versus waiting too long to take more decisive action?
- How should Geordi balance his responsibility to support Julian's development with his responsibility to the team and to Acme's critical infrastructure?
- What role, if any, should the team play in addressing this situation? What about Geordi's manager or PeopleOps?
- If this is a performance issue, what does a performance improvement plan look like for a senior engineer? What if it's not primarily a performance issue?
- How might Geordi's management style of high autonomy and trust be influencing this situation - both in how the problem developed and how it should be resolved?
- What should success look like three months from now? Six months from now?
