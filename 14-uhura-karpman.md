# Context

Uhura manages Acme Corp's Mobile Engineering team, a group of five engineers building the mobile applications that allow banks and partners to interact with CarboniChain. The team has been under pressure for the past six months to deliver a major redesign of the mobile app, codenamed Phoenix, which is critical for Acme's expansion into consumer-facing products.

Scotty, a mid-level engineer on the team, has been the primary developer on Phoenix. From the beginning, Scotty expressed concerns about the timeline, telling Uhura repeatedly that the requirements from the product team were "completely unrealistic" and that he was being "set up to fail." He pointed out that the design specs kept changing, that he wasn't getting enough support from the backend team, and that the testing infrastructure was inadequate. Uhura listened sympathetically and agreed many of his concerns were valid.

As deadlines approached and Scotty continued to fall behind, Uhura stepped in to help more directly. She started attending all of Scotty's meetings with the product team to "run interference" and push back on requirement changes. She personally debugged complex issues in Scotty's code during evenings and weekends. She shielded Scotty from critical feedback in team meetings, explaining to others that he was dealing with "impossible circumstances." When Spock, the product manager, raised concerns about missed milestones, Uhura defended Scotty vigorously, arguing that Spock's requirements were unreasonable and the real problem was lack of backend support from other teams.

Initially, this approach seemed to work. Scotty expressed gratitude for Uhura's support and protection. However, the pattern continued. Each sprint, Scotty would commit to deliverables, encounter problems, and Uhura would jump in to rescue the situation. Scotty became increasingly dependent on Uhura's interventions and less likely to solve problems independently or seek help from teammates.

Meanwhile, Spock grew increasingly frustrated. In a recent stakeholder meeting, he openly criticized the Mobile Engineering team's "constant excuses" and questioned whether they had the right people on Phoenix. Uhura felt attacked and responded defensively, pointing out all the obstacles her team was facing and suggesting that product management should "understand engineering realities better."

The situation has deteriorated further. Last week, after yet another missed milestone, Uhura had a heated 1:1 with Scotty where her frustration boiled over. She told him that she'd "bent over backwards to support him" and that he needed to "step up and deliver." Scotty was visibly shaken and told other team members he felt "thrown under the bus" after being promised support.

Now Chekov, a senior engineer on the team, has come to Uhura expressing concern about Scotty's well-being. Chekov said Scotty seems demoralized and is talking about looking for other jobs. Chekov suggested that maybe Uhura has been too hard on Scotty, and that the real problem is Spock's unrealistic demands. Chekov has started taking on some of Scotty's work to help him out.

The team dynamics have become toxic. Team members are taking sides - some sympathizing with Scotty as overwhelmed and unsupported, others frustrated that he's not being held accountable. Spock has started CCing leadership on emails about Phoenix delays. Uhura feels caught in the middle, angry at Spock for being unreasonable, guilty about losing patience with Scotty, and increasingly resentful that she's working nights and weekends on a project that should be Scotty's responsibility.

# Challenge

How did this situation become so dysfunctional, and how can Uhura break out of this pattern? The team seems stuck in a cycle where everyone is frustrated but the underlying issues never get resolved. What needs to change, and where should Uhura start?

# Discussion Prompts

- Map out the roles different people are playing in this situation. How do those roles interact and reinforce each other?
- How did Uhura's well-intentioned support for Scotty potentially contribute to the current dysfunction?
- What might Scotty be getting out of remaining in a "struggling" position? What might Uhura be getting out of the "rescuer" role?
- How has the conflict between Uhura and Spock evolved? Are they actually addressing the real problems, or is something else happening?
- What happens when Chekov steps in to help Scotty? Is this likely to improve or worsen the situation?
- If you were observing this team from the outside, what patterns would you notice that the people involved might not see?
- What would it look like for Uhura to exit her current role in this dynamic? What specific behaviors would she need to stop and start?
- How might Scotty, Spock, and Chekov each need to change their behaviors for the team to function more healthily?
- What organizational or structural factors might be enabling or perpetuating these dynamics?
- If Uhura tried to have a direct conversation with Scotty about these patterns, how might that go? What could make it productive versus defensive?
- Are there similar patterns in other teams or organizations you've observed? What made them hard to break?
